# Reproducibility audit of the existing manuscript and inventories

Audit snapshot: 2026-11-05. The historical automated citation audit read the then-active manuscript sections, including the former `appendix_inventory.tex`, omitting TeX comments and `comment` environments. Its scripts and results are preserved as a historical audit; their original manuscript paths refer to that earlier version. For the current manuscript or a standalone copy of this directory, use `../revision/citation_audit.py` as explained in `../revision/README.md`. A matched identifier establishes a possible bibliographic relation, not that a work is an eligible core study.

In this generated snapshot, 120 unique citation keys were found, all present in `refs.bib`; 34 keys have candidate links and 86 have no link found. A second file, `citation_search_mapping.csv`, maps citations directly to identifier-level retrieval records from the separate newer search snapshot; 26 keys have links. Most unmatched citations are foundations or adjacent context and are not necessarily search omissions. The matching is conservative: different titles, missing identifiers and multiple versions can produce false negatives; textual title collisions can produce false positives. Numbers will change when the manuscript or candidate file changes.

`unmatched_citation_scope_triage.csv` is a manual *provisional* grouping of the 86 currently unlinked keys: 54 foundations/background, 30 adjacent/transferable, and two OS-target LLM citations that merit priority source review. Recreate it with `python3 reproduct/audit/triage_citations.py` after the citation map. `complete_citation_scope_map.csv` adds provisional citation role, cited paragraph snippets, candidate IDs, later-search IDs and source-checked links for all 120 citations; its generator is `build_complete_citation_map.py`. These labels organize follow-up only. They are not inclusion or exclusion decisions and are not an independent review.

`priority_source_checks.md` describes seven priority items with direct primary-source links. `verified_priority_checks.csv`, generated by `build_priority_checks.py`, preserves the existing candidate fields alongside proposed corrections. Examples include MigGPT L038, where the short score rationale contradicts the published paper's CVE backporting benchmark; the cross-version KEN/Kgent links L041/C0130; and SyzAgent, which was absent from the 225 candidate list but retrieved as R06009 by the later arXiv search. CrashFixer/kAgent and Selene use changed titles in the candidate list. String-only matching must not be used to infer that these studies are absent.

## Screening quality audit

`known_study_retention.csv` and `check_seed_retention.py` check a purposive, source-verified set of seven cited works against the later snapshot and first-pass triage. SyzAgent and kAgent were retained as possible core. The raw model excluded KEN's preprint and MigGPT, although the published MigGPT paper also reports CVE patch backporting; a subsequent conservative OS-keyword guard restored both to UNCLEAR. Kgent's published version was also UNCLEAR. Three other seed studies had no exact or source-verified match in the frozen search snapshot. Missing from retrieval is not a screening error. These deliberately selected cases are not a representative sensitivity estimate.

`screening_audit_sample_manifest.json` records the fixed seed 20261105 and hashes for a 60-record stratified sample of the 6,408 completed **original search snapshot** records, with 10 sampled per raw decision and abstract-availability stratum. The exact sampled input is preserved as `screening_results_sample_frame.csv`; the search input is `../search_protocol/records.csv`. `draw_screening_sample.py` reconstructs the sample from these inputs and refuses to overwrite an existing sample. `screening_audit_review.csv` records a separate Codex model's judgment and reason for each title/abstract. The first model's labels and reasons were visible to that reviewer; it is a non-blinded audit, not independent screening or a human review. Against that frozen frame 53 effective decisions agree and seven differ; this deliberately stratified sample is too small and nonproportional for a population error-rate claim. All ten no-abstract raw exclusions in the sample were already conservatively changed to UNCLEAR by the first guard.

The subsequent OS-keyword guard changed the effective decision for R01286 from EXCLUDE to UNCLEAR. `screening_audit_current_comparison.csv`, its hash manifest, and `screening_audit_manual_review_queue.csv` compare this audit with the later guarded result. The seven candidate disagreements require adjudication; conservatively retaining an item as UNCLEAR is not itself a verified error. These quality checks do not cover the separately retrieved arXiv pagination extension.

The saved sample was regenerated from the archived frame and `records.csv` in a temporary copy; its CSV was byte-identical. The separately generated `../screening/high_priority_review_queue.csv` contains all seven sampled candidate disagreements and all five seed records retrieved by the original search snapshot. Its rows are pending review, not completed eligibility decisions.

## Missing-abstract follow-up

`missing_abstract_crosssource.csv` checks the 37 no-abstract items in the frozen high-priority queue against the saved search snapshot, paginated arXiv results, and earlier 225-candidate inventory. Its reproducible local linkage distinguishes 12 matches, five title-only ambiguous links and 20 with no match found. Only four matches yield an actual abstract already saved in the input snapshot; existing candidate summaries and fragments are explicitly labelled and must not be represented as verbatim abstracts. Match status describes work linkage and does not make an eligibility decision. The alternatives file records every possible local match; the manifest contains hashes of the input snapshots.

`missing_abstract_online_checks.csv` separately records a follow-up check of the 25 missing or ambiguous local links on 5 November 2026 UTC. Nine primary sources were directly accessible on recheck; four publisher abstracts were seen in indexed results but their direct pages subsequently blocked access; one previously indexed paper PDF returned HTTP 502 on recheck, while its conference program still identified the title and DOI; eleven had no primary abstract obtained. Each record supplies the URL, identity evidence, method, and a short paraphrase when verified. The web tool did not record exact query times. The paraphrases are not original abstracts and must not be presented as such in later screening. A failed search or blocked page does not prove the paper is nonexistent. The script `build_missing_abstract_online_checks.py` compiles these manual checks; it does not rerun the web searches.

## Main findings

1. **The old retrospective search is not reconstructible from present evidence.** The archive has 485 rows, 220 with historical scores 1–3. The submitted historical supplementary file and record-level exclusion/screening history are missing. Thus neither the 485→220 path nor the exact set previously submitted can be established from the matching score distribution alone.
2. **The 225 merged candidates are not the manuscript's full cited corpus or a unique eligible set.** The old/new crosswalk and the citation map trace different relationships. Examples needing human-verifiable version reconciliation include KEN/Kgent L041/C0130, and the manuscript's cited SyzAgent and SimpleBPF do not match an entry in the 225 candidate snapshot. Merge or supplementation must be documented, not silently relabeled as a database hit.
3. **Record-level rapid-screen decisions are insufficient to reproduce a final selection.** The archived merge script stores manually selected ranks from a ranked 200; it does not provide validated individual screening rationales for all 1,454 original DOI-deduplicated candidate rows, independent duplicate ratings, or full-text exclusions. The original query metadata stores raw Google Scholar-style terms, whereas the Crossref script transforms them to loose bibliographic keywords. The supplied script preserves the transformation, but the top-20 truncation and 2022 lower bound limit recall.
4. **Some source summaries conflict with their own underlying evidence.** In L038 the short MigGPT score rationale claims no security-specific evaluation, but its own prior memo and the published paper document a CVE patch-backporting benchmark. This cannot be resolved by a title/abstract-only classifier: preserve the source evidence and reassess under explicit eligibility criteria.
5. **Claims, retrieved sources and synthesized evidence need separate provenance fields.** Record per study: canonical study ID, publication/preprint identifiers, database hit or citation-led source, duplicate decision and evidence, title/abstract and source checked, relevance decision and reason, validation status, reviewer identity/tool and decision timestamp, and linked manuscript citation/role. Retain uncertain and missing values. Do not represent an LLM assessment as independent human screening, or a candidate score as a final quality/eligibility result.

Before publication, freeze a release of the search snapshot, study/version crosswalk, decision log and manuscript, compute counts from the frozen records, and publish the actual release URL/DOI. Broader retrieval and independently conducted review can improve coverage and reliability but should be described only if performed and logged.
